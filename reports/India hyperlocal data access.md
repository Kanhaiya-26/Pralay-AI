# Public feeds unlock genuine Indian observations

PRALAY can acquire genuine station-level Indian weather data from official public IMD website feeds even though the tested catalogue APIs require authentication. The strongest immediate sources are the published AWS, SYNOP and METAR point layers, together with official district warning polygons. Their actual coordinates and timestamps support a useful geographic display; shrinking an aggregation grid cannot improve their measurement precision. The public feeds also contain stale and unresolved records, so a successful HTTP response is insufficient evidence of healthy coverage. NASA provides a separate working public IMERG rainfall visualization, while quantitative precipitation access and the credentialed FIRMS, Sentinel Hub and ReliefWeb routes remain access-dependent. These research results establish fetch accessibility and payload structure, not application ingestion or browser verification; those milestones must be reported from the implementation's actual runs.

## IMD public website feeds work where catalogue APIs require authentication

The official IMD index lists **28 requested dataset categories**, but its reference body supplies URLs for 20 datasets and two mappings. The normal unauthenticated GET audit tested those 22 URLs and the 11 supplied legacy candidates: **all 33 returned HTTP 401**. Eight indexed categories had no published API URL in the reference and were left unresolved rather than assigned invented paths. No login, key guessing or IP restriction workaround was attempted. These results mean that the API access method requires authorization; they do not establish that the datasets are absent. The endpoint-by-endpoint response evidence is preserved in `reports/imd_probe.json`. ([IMD reference](https://api.imd.gov.in/public/api_reference.html), [API account portal](https://api.imd.gov.in/public/index.php))

Separately, official rendered IMD pages publish public GeoServer WFS requests. Six exact layers returned **HTTP 200 GeoJSON**: AWS, SYNOP, METAR, station nowcasts, district nowcasts and district warnings. The bounded research responses contained 2,000 AWS features, 466 SYNOP features, 145 METAR features, 1,000 station nowcasts, and 50 features from each district layer. Several responses were capped below their advertised total, so these counts must not be described as complete national coverage. The observation layers provide real station points, while district warning features provide their original administrative polygons. A district polygon remains district-level information; its boundary does not establish street-level hazard exposure. ([Official observation page](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/index_en.php?lang=en), [station nowcast page](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/responsive/stationWiseNowcastGIS.php), [district warning page](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/responsive/districtWiseWarningGIS.php))

## Freshness and country checks determine which coordinates are useful

The public observation feeds mix recent readings with records months or years old. At the research check, the fetched AWS sample contained **982 records within 24 hours and 1,018 older records**; SYNOP contained 417 recent and 49 older records, and METAR 136 recent and nine older records. These are research freshness counts before country, geometry or measurement-quality filtering. AWS serialized time-of-day against a 1970 placeholder date, requiring the source observation date to be combined with that clock. Nowcast issue and expiry fields require explicit timezone handling. All 100 sampled public RSS nowcasts had already expired, despite the RSS request succeeding. ([Observation publisher](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/index_en.php?lang=en), [nowcast publisher](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/responsive/stationWiseNowcastGIS.php), [district RSS](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/dist_nowcast_rss.php))

Country attribution requires more than an India bounding rectangle or an Indian place mentioned in a label. The seven-day bounded USGS query returned **17 earthquake observations**, but this includes neighboring-country and offshore points. One event's nearest-place text mentions Bamboo Flat, India, while the epicentre lies offshore. A global-feed substring filter would also accept the unrelated Indian-Antarctic Ridge. Preserve the instrumental epicentre and its uncertainty; do not move a point to the nearest Indian district. ([USGS bounded query](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1/query?format=geojson&starttime=2026-10-24&endtime=2026-11-01&minlatitude=6&maxlatitude=38&minlongitude=68&maxlongitude=98&limit=100&orderby=time), [event record](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/us7000tty7), [USGS feed specification](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/feed/v1.0/geojson.php))

A publicly downloaded geoBoundaries geometry supplies an approximate country polygon suitable for conservative filtering, but its historical metadata has inconsistencies that must remain documented. This is an operational filter, not an authoritative legal-boundary claim. Border uncertainty should lead to an unresolved record rather than a forced match. ([Boundary source](https://api.github.com/repos/wmgeolab/geoBoundaries/contents/releaseData/gbOpen/IND/ADM0), [metadata](https://media.githubusercontent.com/media/wmgeolab/geoBoundaries/main/releaseData/gbOpen/IND/ADM0/geoBoundaries-IND-ADM0-metaData.json))

## Public IMERG imagery is available without quantitative rainfall access

NASA's public GIBS capabilities advertised `IMERG_Precipitation_Rate_30min` through **31 October 2026 at 09:30 UTC**. A bounded India WMS request returned a valid, nonempty **480 × 512 PNG**, with 20,753 nontransparent pixels. Its exact bbox, CRS, layer and acquisition time support a georeferenced visualization. Public CMR metadata independently returned a real Early Run granule for the same half-hour. Neither result means that numeric precipitation values were downloaded or that rainfall proves a flood. Display resolution must remain separate from the native product's resolution. ([GIBS access](https://nasa-gibs.github.io/gibs-api-docs/access-basics/), [tested capabilities](https://gibs.earthdata.nasa.gov/wmts/epsg4326/best/1.0.0/WMTSCapabilities.xml), [IMERG metadata query](https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/granules.json?short_name=GPM_3IMERGHHE&version=07&bounding_box=68,6,98,38&page_size=1&sort_key=-start_date))

The tested Early GIS directory returned **401**, while a metadata-advertised OPeNDAP resource redirected to Earthdata login. The official GIS route also requires PPS registration; Earthdata credentials must not be assumed to authorize a separate PPS account automatically. The public daily GIBS layer is a precipitation-rate visualization, not an accumulation product. Deriving accumulated rainfall from a color screenshot would fabricate numerical information. Quantitative Early/Late subsets and accumulated products therefore need a separately validated authenticated access method, units and processing description. ([NASA IMERG access](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/imerg), [PPS registration notes](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/directory), [tested GIS directory](https://jsimpsonhttps.pps.eosdis.nasa.gov/imerg/gis/early/))

## Credentialed and context sources need separate success milestones

All six requested mandatory-provider credential variables were absent during the access audit. FIRMS Area API requires a registered MAP_KEY and supports bounded MODIS/VIIRS queries; the API adapter must not label documentation-page success as fire-detection success. Sentinel Hub Catalog and the documented Process endpoint returned **401 without a bearer token**. Its backend OAuth flow can cache temporary tokens and retrieve bounded radar/optical products once authorized, but neither an image nor a scene footprint is a confirmed flood map. ReliefWeb returned **400, “Missing appname parameter”**; an approved appname is a prerequisite for its read requests. ([FIRMS API](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/api/area/), [Sentinel authentication](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/APIs/SentinelHub/Overview/Authentication.html), [Catalog API](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/APIs/SentinelHub/Catalog.html), [Process examples](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/APIs/SentinelHub/Process/Examples/S1GRD.html), [ReliefWeb requirements](https://apidoc.reliefweb.int/))

SACHET returned current RSS and CAP warning documents, but the three sampled CAP payloads supplied no polygon or circle. `status=Actual` identifies a real alert rather than a test message; the same alerts can describe expected hazards with possible or likely certainty. WorldPop returned **21 historical population metadata records for 2000–2020**, useful as exposure context rather than live disaster observations. Google News and Mastodon returned public items, but successful retrieval does not establish that they concern an actual Indian disaster or provide independent corroboration. ([SACHET RSS](https://sachet.ndma.gov.in/cap_public_website/rss/rss_india.xml), [sample CAP](https://sachet.ndma.gov.in/cap_public_website/FetchXMLFile?identifier=1793455917187017), [WorldPop India catalogue](https://www.worldpop.org/rest/data/pop/wpgp?iso3=IND), [Mastodon public method](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/methods/timelines/#tag))

Other tested routes remain constrained: Bluesky public search returned **403**, GDELT **429**, and bounded Overpass requests timed out. The EMS activations page returned HTML but no validated India geospatial product. These results require independent source-health states and retry policies, not a common green “connected” indicator. ([Bluesky endpoint](https://public.api.bsky.app/xrpc/app.bsky.feed.searchPosts?q=India%20flood&limit=10), [GDELT endpoint](https://api.gdeltproject.org/api/v2/doc/doc?query=India%20flood&mode=artlist&format=json&maxrecords=5&timespan=1d), [Overpass documentation](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API), [EMS activations](https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/activations/))

## Conclusion

The meaningful upgrade is a provenance-preserving acquisition model that can show fresh station points and genuine warning geometry while keeping unresolved locations out of precise map layers. A finer aggregation grid is useful for inspection, but only source geometry, source resolution and timestamp validation can justify the detail shown inside it. Country-wide discovery should query all configured relevant sources, return their failures alongside results, and distinguish observations, unverified occurrence reports, warnings, forecasts and historical context.

Deployment confidence must be earned in stages: an implemented adapter, valid authentication, a successful live fetch, accepted normalized records and an inspected GIS display are different achievements. The implementation completion report should supply those actual counts and tests; this research deliberately does not convert accessibility probes into ingestion or display claims.
